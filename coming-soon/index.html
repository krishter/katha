Stories don't vanish all at once. They fade a detail at a time — not because no one cares, but because no one asked.

Katha asks.

A warm voice on WhatsApp that listens whenever your parent feels like talking, in their language — and keeps every story they tell.

The days

She talks. Katha remembers.

A week later, Katha picks up a thread she left. Not a quiz, just the next question a good listener would ask.

And before she goes, Katha tells her what it has kept safe.

Her story, her say

Something worth sharing takes shape.

From what she's told Katha, it picks one thing you might not know, in her words, translated into English.

  • “…the river house, near the old bridge…”
  • “I was nine. Or ten?”
  • “seven of us children”
  • “wash your feet first!”
  • “the mango tree by the gate”
  • “Appa's cycle bell”

Illustrative

Katha reads it to her first, in her language. No card is sent to you until she says yes. Your family can't change a word. It's her story. It stays hers.

Then it reaches you.

By email and on WhatsApp, a few hours before you usually call. Up to twice a week.

For US numbers, by email only for now.

You call. You ask.

Or reply with a question of your own. Katha will ask her in a coming conversation, and tell her it came from you.

If she'd like to share her answer, it comes back to you the same way — once she's said yes.
